# V3.2 whole-memory review: broad retention, useful voice, modest economy, an unresolved revision verdict

The wider reading is more favorable than the earlier failure-focused account.
V3.2 retains the substance and expressive character of this conversation. It
does not show a general collapse in facts, ideas, voice or continuity. It also
uses somewhat less text and output. Its clearest loss against V3.1 is the
first-build priority in one repetition. Its proposed repair improvement is
still not demonstrated, and this conversation is a weak test of the new revise
system's original purpose: repairing stale, differently worded existing claims.

I would keep V3.2 as a contender for the wider comparison. This review does not
establish that it beats V3.1 overall, or production. It does reduce the case for
another prompt revision devoted mainly to polishing attribution in this one
design conversation.

## Scope and method

“Last round” here means the completed nine-encode V3.2 sanity: three sequential
five-pair windows, repeated three times on Sonnet 4.6. The comparator is the
nine saved V3.1 encodes on the same 15-pair creative-design conversation. These
are repetitions of one source, not nine independent conversations. V3.1 was
recorded earlier; provider/runtime drift is not fully controlled.
The source is a repository synthetic conversation whose user is named Tom;
the preferences below are evidence about that fixture, not newly observed
facts about the real operator. “Assistant” means the source's assistant.

I reread all 52 final V3.2 nodes, their populated text fields, quotes and
outgoing relation descriptions; all nine carried continuities; the actual
field changes and call order. For V3.1, this supplements the earlier full
review with content and targeted complete-node comparisons, all nine Arc
lines, revision deltas and graph structure. This is my retrospective reading,
not a blind judge or another model run. The earlier report remains unchanged.

The unit of credit is recoverable knowledge in the whole memory. A useful
quote can preserve sentiment or a concrete detail absent from the paraphrase.
I separately identify ambiguity that remains in another field and complete
loss. The table below is a source coverage review, not a new benchmark score.
No production prompt, candidate or source fixture was edited.

## 2. Voice and sentiment: stronger than the earlier defect list suggested

All three V3.2 repeats retain the recognizable phrases “brain breathing,”
“mirror, not a camera,” “peripheral vision for knowledge,” and the beauty versus
spreadsheet framing. The corresponding memories are not sterile summaries.
The assistant's contributions also survive: implementation proposals, color and sound
mapping, the agency distinction and the ambient-intelligence expansion.
There is no evidence here of fixing user-fact capture by suppressing my voice.

The strongest example is the ambient-presence memory. In V3.2 repeat 3,
`7a1664a0` retains Tom's own framing, the assistant's menubar/notification proposals, the
non-interruption intent and the fact that those mechanisms are unconfirmed.
It gives a future reader both the feeling and a concrete starting point.
The same separation is visible in repeats 1 and 2. V3.1 repeat 1 instead calls
the mechanisms agreed in situation; repeat 2 reasons that ending the thread
amounted to affirmation. This is a useful local improvement in V3.2.

The aesthetic case should receive **retention credit with attribution
ambiguity**. Repeat 2 `ea20fc3c` and repeat 3 `0e872ceb` misattribute the assistant's
aesthetic-product sentence to Tom in prose, but preserve both speakers'
actual quotes. The central design requirement is recoverable and expressive.
That is materially different from losing the prototype priority entirely.
The ownership defect matters more when repeat 3 copies it into the personal
profile and describes it as evidence about Tom's identity; a reader of that
profile alone has less help from the original quote.

Similarly, repeat 1's theming reasoning calls the four names mine, while
`their_raw_quote` correctly contains Tom's enumeration. Keep the four-theme
and voice credit; report the conflicting attribution without describing the
themes as lost. The earlier quote-exactness checks also remain descriptive:
one nonmatching quote in each arm is not a whole-memory quality score.

## 3. Arcs: continuity works, but decisions get less emphasis than topic inventory

Every V3.2 window supplies a nonempty Arc line, and the three lines accumulate
in each repeat's saved continuity. They preserve the movement:

1. Living graph, attention/temperature, gaps and encoder transparency.
2. Temporal growth, session impact, health and personal-reflection agency.
3. Themes and sound expand into ambient presence, followed by prototype and
   aesthetic direction.

Repeat 3 explicitly captures the conceptual extension: “Tom's vision now
reaches beyond the dashboard into a persistent ambient layer.” That is a
real arc, not merely a feature name. None of the three repeats loses the whole
earlier phase when the next window arrives.

The limit is emphasis. Much Arc wording describes “nodes encoded,” “features
added,” or “spec folded” rather than what the collaborators decided and why.
All three V3.2 closing Arc lines mention a prototype but omit its explicit
graph/temperature-first priority. The nodes rescue that information in repeats
2 and 3. V3.1's third repeat names the priority in its Arc; its other repeats
also lean toward encoding inventories. This is a shared weakness with a
concrete V3.2 regression in repeat 1, not evidence of an absent Arc mechanism.

The actual contract asks for one line of **new movement** per window, so a
full narrative retelling is not the target. Nor does this three-window
conversation test long-separated life arcs or distinguishing two similar
events; the earlier gift-corpus evidence remains relevant for those abilities.

These are the existing saved-delta counts, including enrichment. They are not
quality points. There was little source-driven reversal of an earlier fact,
so “fewer changed fields” cannot establish that the revise system regressed.

Useful V3.2 revisions include:

- Repeat 1 adds D3 and field mappings to the existing graph node while keeping
  weighted distance, pulse, glow and the organic-design rationale.
- Repeat 2 updates the mirror node's reasoning with Tom's explicit later
  endorsement, and extends graph reasoning with the prototype context.
- Repeat 3 evolves the personal profile through all three windows and extends
  the graph node with the proposed implementation library.

V3.1 expands its original vision node in every repeat. V3.2 instead leaves
the original vision relatively focused in repeats 1/2 and supplies linked
feature/ambient nodes; repeat 3 develops a profile as its organizing node.
Either organization can retain the knowledge. A vision hub does not have to
be rewritten into an exhaustive feature list to count as successful revision.

The real preservation failure is repeat 1's consolidation choice: `new`
recognizes the prototype and chooses to fold it into `492707b8`; the write
retains implementation details but omits the first-build priority. There is
no compensating quote, node or Arc entry in the final memory.

No V3.2 window makes a semantic repair after its initial write. The saved
V3.1 chains likewise contain no second-pass text revision; their extra writes
are connect batches. V3.2's repair example therefore has no demonstrated gain
here. This is not a reason to require two writes on every run. The historical
stale-field/propagation corpus is a much stronger discriminator for the new
revise system than these mostly additive design windows.

## 5. Behavior and synthesis: present, but not thoroughly tested

The memories retain that Tom cares about meaningful visual form, wants to
inspect the agent's judgment, and treats personal reflection as powerful but
sensitive. Those observations are useful for future collaboration. Repeat 3
creates and twice enriches `2409bfd7`, a profile joining aesthetic preference,
privacy sensitivity and ambient vision. That demonstrates willingness to
carry understanding of the person alongside implementation facts.

It also illustrates the tradeoff: the profile bundles project-specific
requirements with broader character language, and its aesthetic attribution
is overconfident. Most of its substance remains grounded in this discussion,
but it is not independent evidence of a general personality trait.

Useful synthesis is not confined to `thought`. Repeat 3's session-impact edge
to the health map separates **investment over time** from **current condition**;
its time-lapse edge distinguishes continuous evolution from session aggregates.
Repeat 1's health/gap connection distinguishes absent knowledge from known but
neglected knowledge. These are useful derived relationships even though no
one dictated them as finished sentences.

V3.1 also contributes a particularly useful distinction in repeat 2:
`409a2144` explains that cold/dormant knowledge can still be correct, unlike
stale/wrong knowledge. V3.2 does not preserve that explicit distinction in
this form. This is lost **additional synthesis**, not an omitted source fact,
and evidence against treating V3.2 as uniformly richer.

This source contains explicit design preferences, not much evidence about
commit habits, testing sensitivity or repeated interpersonal correction.
Those original objectives remain untested by the last round; their absence
from this source must not be scored as an encoder failure.

## 7. Edges and organization: useful relationships, some duplication and reach risk

Stored outgoing relations total 84 → 69. This cannot be read directly as
either a quality loss or better selectivity. The same saved graph-orientation
caveat from the original evaluation still applies; no recall sweep ran here.

There are meaningful relationships beyond “belongs to dashboard,” as noted
above. There is also avoidable repetition: repeat 2 links audio to ambient
presence twice, under `grounds` and `supports`, with essentially the same
explanation. Repeat 1 applies personal-inference governance to ordinary
theming without a corresponding personal-data feature in the source; the
description is conditional, but the chosen connection is weakly grounded.

A new saved-graph check finds final nodes with no semantic neighbor in
V3.2 repeats 1/2/3: **1 / 1 / 0**. They are the main journal overlay in repeat
1 (`c4b6387c`) and the missing-spec open in repeat 2 (`0a1f9421`). V3.1 is
**0 / 2 / 0**: repeat 2's gap and journal-overlay nodes. This counts undirected
adjacency among the final semantic nodes, not automatic community links or
retrieval success. V3.2 does not broadly solve or worsen isolation by this
small count, but its main journal node's isolation is a plausible access risk.

Repeat 3's personal profile becomes a hub for many project features. That can
make the session coherent, but it may blur project knowledge and understanding
of Tom during later retrieval. This remains a design tradeoff to measure,
not an observed recall failure.

## 8. Residue and remaining consequential ambiguity

All nine V3.2 windows complete normally, create nodes and close; none is a
token-limit failure or an empty encoding. Two first windows read before
writing. No new API or partial-tool failure occurred.

Review notes preserve legitimate uncertainty: unlocated journal design,
unconfirmed feature mechanisms, and the implementation of edit/delete
controls. Repeat 3's question about deleting an inference embedded in a
correction node is a useful implementation concern. Such doubt is value,
not necessarily procrastination.

Some notes are noisy or misleading. Repeat 1 marks the journal item resolved
while saying it remains open; repeat 3 retires the ask while keeping its doubt.
Retiring a note does not itself claim completion of the underlying design,
and the unknown state remains visible. This is weaker evidence of damaging
deferral than the historical no-mint residue loop. Repeat 2's suggestion that
four unrelated dashboard features deepen their dependency on the missing
journal spec invents an unnecessary dependency in continuity.

The remaining issues have different practical weights:

| Issue | Whole-memory consequence |
|---|---|
| Prototype first-build priority missing in V3.2 R1 | Material loss; no other stored carrier rescues the choice. |
| Optional/quiet audio becomes off-by-default in R1/R3 | Original wording survives in quotes, but implementation guidance is stronger. V3.1 R2 also does this. Count ambiguity, not complete loss. |
| D3 proposal called agreed in R2 situation | The useful proposal and raw source survive, but a future implementation reader could infer an unmade commitment. R3 is more careful about the library. |
| Aesthetic/theme ownership errors with correct quotes | Sentiment and detailed knowledge remain usable. Lower weight for expressing the design; higher weight when used as evidence about the person's own words. |
| Unimplemented ideas, tentative mechanisms, genuine unknowns | Appropriate knowledge when kept at that status; not defects merely because they are unresolved. |

## Overall assessment and next decision

V3.2 retains the broad capabilities we wanted to protect in this slice:
specific information, meaning, both voices, developing continuity and useful
connections. There is a modest economy benefit and some better separation of
vision from implementation proposals. There is no established overall win
over V3.1: the prototype-priority loss, additional-synthesis tradeoff and
absence of demonstrated repair benefit weigh against claiming one.

The next discriminating evidence is the broader production/candidate
comparison, particularly historical stale-claim propagation, first-disclosure
facts and downstream recall. This review supports carrying the frozen V3.2
contender into that decision rather than automatically authoring V3.3 from
the familiar aesthetic example. It does not authorize promotion or establish
a Sonnet 4.6 capability ceiling.

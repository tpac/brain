### What the transfer cell says

- **The revision and fidelity gains transfer.** On material no arm had seen,
  V3.3 revised changed claims in place with the widest field walk, held
  interest-not-commitment, decision-not-act and recommendation-not-fact
  apart, detected the one arithmetically impossible update, and served the
  retrieved-subset reader best (14 of 15 downstream, 26 of 36 receiver's
  view). This is what the revise infra and the semantic-fidelity challenge
  were built for, and it holds outside the development source.
- **Two costs are real, specific and not arm-noise.** Coverage on
  assistant-heavy Q&A windows (15 of 42 windows at two nodes or fewer, one
  session gated out, whole recommendation turns dropped in two or three of
  three repeats) and the quote field standing outside the revision walk (1
  refresh in 31 events; the superseded number left in the quote three of
  three times). Both are the kind of loss Tom's rule addresses by weaving,
  not adding: the worked second window already shows a fact moving through
  title, content, situation and reasoning — the quote that carried the old
  number is the surface it does not touch; and no worked window shows a
  recommendation-heavy exchange and what "useful" means there (the offered
  set, the chosen subset, the reason).
- **One infra finding stands on its own.** A refused multi-swap revise applies
  nothing and its error does not say so; the encoder read "swap[1]" as "the
  second one" and certified a revision that never landed. The write-boundary
  fix is a clause in the `apply_swaps` error; the eval measured the frozen
  message, so nothing is changed here.
- **Thought did not move, in either cell.** Two populated thoughts in 148
  nodes. The paragraph's restoration is not what produces the field.
- **Limits.** One reviewer per pack; strong/adequate/weak tallied as a
  reading aid; three repeats per cell, so per-corpus swings rest on three
  points; one held-out question per LongMemEval item and none for the
  synthetic source; the Hawaii question's "stay" is ambiguous and the V3.3

  reviewers saw whole memories plus a probe, not a live recall session.
  Nothing here is merged, activated, restarted or deployed.

What the sweep establishes about the infrastructure:

- **Landing, preservation, deltas, revised_at.** Every successful op landed
  exactly as composed — 32 / 28 / 31 node-windows on transfer, 7 / 7 on
  sanity; every field no op touched is byte-identical before and after; the
  returned deltas match the actual change; `revised_at` moves only when
  title or content changed. No defect found in 104 transfer and 16 sanity
  operations.
- **Vectors.** For every revised node, the stored embedding text of every
  single-field and blend vector is the current field text (223 / 202 / 226
  vectors checked, none stale, none missing), and every vector whose source
  field changed in the node's last revise was re-created at or after the
  node's `updated_at` (153 / 123 / 129 checked, none unrefreshed). The
  invalidate-and-requeue path works; the legacy `_situation` vector stores no
  text and is verified by timestamp only.
- **Refusals are one encoder error, not many.** Nine of nine refused swap
  ops on transfer failed because `old` was not in the field named: in eight
  the sentence lives in `reasoning` or `situation` of the same node (the
  encoder sent a reasoning sentence as a content swap), one was a no-op swap
  with identical old and new. V3.2 sent four swap ops and none was refused.
  After a refusal the encoder returned to the same field with a later op in
  16 of 18 production intents and 10 of 15 V3.3 intents; the one that did not
  land at all is the Nightingale case above.
- **connect_to on revise** was used only by V3.3: three ops on transfer,
  every requested edge present afterwards with its why; one op on sanity
  with four entries, all refused — the encoder passed sibling titles as
  `target`, and on revise `target` must be an existing node id. The tool's
  refusal says so; the prompt's worked window shows an id.

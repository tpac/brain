## Position

**V3.5 is not an improvement on V3.4, and it is not adopted.** On the fresh cell it loses the
downstream answer test (10 against 12 of 15), loses the question lane in two of three repeats
(35 / 79 / 33% fill against V3.4's 61 / 59 / 63%), doubles the isolates, and writes two whole
sequences with no node at all; on the blind read it trails V3.4 by a wide margin (90 to 121 of 192, twelve packs, two
repeats, V3.4 ahead on seven of eight dimensions) and edges production (80). What it wins is the thought lane (10 thoughts against 2, all
hunches by the judge's reading), generic relations (4 against 9) and near-twins (4 against 7), and
two ledger repairs that were correct on their face. The three cross-arm defect classes it was woven
for did not move by their own instruments. The one change that measurably worked — naming `thought`
at the gist's recency slot — is also the leading hypothesis for what destabilized the question lane:
the gist's `new`-line sentence now ends on `thought`, and the question item sits mid-list. That is a
trade, not a gain, and it needs an ablation arm (V3.5 template with V3.4's gist) before anything is
attributed. The regression sets say the same thing more quietly: on the twelve development corpora
V3.5 is level with V3.4 on the census, worse on downstream (9 and 10 against 12 and 12), and more
overclaimed by the per-node judge on the V3.4 six (18% against 9%).

The lesson is the one the September 8 probes already recorded (id:dbac6cab, id:4ea780f0): prose
rules do not move behaviour on unseen material; position and examples do, and position trades one
lane for another. Six of V3.5's eleven changes were clauses (X1, X2, X3, X4, W7, W8, W10); none
moved its readout. The two that landed on position (X5, X6) moved their lane and cost another. The
one example change (W6a) did not reach the class it targeted (values left in quotes rose on the
development set). V3.4 stays the standing candidate.

## What this buys the next step

1. **V3.4 remains the candidate.** V3.5 is a measured lesson, not a version: keep W9 and W6c (the two
   ledger repairs, zero-risk text fixes) for whatever follows; drop the clause weaves.
2. **The thought lever is real and it has a price.** Test it alone: V3.4 + X5 + X6 against V3.4 with
   the gist as the only axis, three repeats, question fill and downstream as the readouts. If the
   question lane holds, the lever is free; if not, the roll-call needs both fields at its tail.
3. **The zero-encode class is the largest single loss in the fresh cell** (six of 54 sequences across
   arms; two packs decided by it) and no clause reached it. Its carrier is procedural, not prose: the
   `new` list judged "generic Q&A" before the first-disclosure rule ran. That is the guide-shaped
   fix the September 8 work already argued for (fact-first rule ahead of the worth judgment,
   id:06fef26a), and it belongs in the gist's `new` paragraph as a procedure, with an example whose
   input looks like a Q&A session and whose output is three plain facts.
4. **The instruments held.** The stale-surface detector, the firming census and the per-node judge
   each said where to read and were confirmed or overturned by the blind read in the expected places
   (the judge's overclaim list matches the reviewers' probe-3 findings; the detector's quote hits are
   the W1 class the reviewers named; the firming census's word count did not track the judge's
   semantic read, so it stays a queue). They run on every future cell at zero or near-zero cost.

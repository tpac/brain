Embedding & Recall Investigation

Can a better embedder (or another lever) fix the brain's recall? Findings from a head-to-head benchmark on our own corpus, current-SOTA research, and reconciliation with the RECALL-SR-REDESIGN.md arc. torch phase deferred · selector is next

TL;DR. The brain is a HippoRAG-class semantic-graph memory bottlenecked at a flat embedder — nomic resolves topic, not proposition-level relevance, so the right node sits buried among topical siblings. On that flat embedder, every graph/edge/field re-combination we've tested is ≤ plain cosine (PPR, semantic-PPR, query→edge seeding, the 11-vector blend, cosine re-ranking — all dead). Only two levers survive, plus one untested probe: (1) a multi-cue SELECTOR that recovers ~3× the signal already present on today's embedder (22%→53% ceiling); (2) a sharper EMBEDDER that lifts every lane at once; and (3) a cross-encoder reranker — categorically different (joint attention), so the "cosine-rerank is dead" result doesn't touch it. Untested.

1 · The problem, quantified

The flat space is the disease. Pure cue→node cosine (nomic-Q, 73-cue teacher gold): hit@5 ~22% · hit@25 ~37% · top-1 cos 0.79, top-25 0.74 → spread 0.05 · the gold node sits on average 0.069 cosine *below* the top non-gold (gold_z 2.2). The right answer is usually in the pile, not at the top. Even the oracle — embedding the actual next turn as a perfect query — caps at hit@5 42% / hit@25 68% (relevance signal ~0.00–0.05 < embedding jitter ~0.05–0.09 → near-random sort within the band). oracle ceiling & flat-space finding: sibling streams (RECALL-SR arc, nodes 836f5d6d / dea1a002).

Two miss classes (route fixes differently): (A) within-cluster smear — 26% of misses, gold within 0.03 of the top-5 bar → an embedding / reranker lever. (B) cue-far — 53% never in the pool at all → a query / seed lever, not embedder-fixable. node adb91eb7.

2 · Methodology

Corpus

73 cues (endo_gold_corpus.json), teacher-labeled essential+helpful gold. Frozen 5,946-node snapshot. Per-cue cutoff (no future leakage).

Ranker — model-isolating

Pure single-vector cosine on title+content (_primary). Every model runs the identical pipeline — only the model varies. Each gets its documented prefix.

Metrics

retrieval: hit@{1,5,25}, recall@5, nDCG@5. discrimination (the lever): spread, gold_z, gold-margin. cost: latency, RAM.

Three honest caveats. (1) Gold-bias → gains are a LOWER bound — the gold pool was nomic+FTS-generated, so a better model's novel finds can't be credited. (2) Flat-space noise — same model re-embedded moves hit@5 ±3% (gold sits 0.003 below the cut), so compare within-sweep and weight the stable discrimination metrics. (3) Cost metrics under shared load — latency/RAM are relative; final numbers from a quiet window.

An engineering note worth keeping: bulk-embedding our corpus required length-aware budget batching — attention is O(seq²)·batch, and our 40–7,800-char nodes spiked RSS to 45 GB at batch=64. Capping batch×max-seq bounds it to ~1.9 GB, full content kept. (enable_mem_pattern=False alone — the daemon's slow-leak fix — does not bound this; different mechanism.)

6 · "Specialized negative" embeddings

Negation-aware (marginal)

Standard embeddings are negation-blind ("X" ≈ "not X"). Fixes exist (negation adapters, DEO). But our disease is sibling-closeness, not negation queries — minor lever for us.

Hard-negative discriminative (the real one)

"Distinguishing hard-negative pairs of extreme similarity substantially improves discrimination — esp. for near-duplicates" = exactly gold-vs-siblings. 2026 SOTA bakes this in (NV-Retriever positive-aware mining, NV-Embed, LGAI). It's why newer models may sharpen our space, and what our fine-tuning would do.

7 · HippoRAG 2 — the brain's mirror

HippoRAG (RAG + Knowledge Graph + Personalized PageRank, neurobiologically-inspired LLM memory) is, almost exactly, the brain's architecture — published, open-source, externally validated. Its ablations (MuSiQue Recall@5): query→triple linking +20.9, passage-node integration +11, LLM triple-filter +1.7. It runs on NV-Embed-v2 (7B).

8 · What the Selector is

A small learned ranker (regularized logistic / learning-to-rank) over the per-(cue, candidate-node) feature vector — all the weak-but-complementary signals: cue×field cosines, FTS, episodic, graph 1-hop-from-context, prev-turn cosine. It learns which signal to trust when, instead of averaging.

Why it's the top lever. Any single signal ≈ 19–22% hit@5, but the per-gold best-of-all-signals = 53% / 64% — the cues are strongly complementary; the brain extracts ~⅓ of what's already there. Naive averaging dilutes back to ~22% (one real signal drowned by ten noisy ones) — the selector is what beats the average. sibling §16, node 8bcc8c96.
The catch: 53% is a best-feature-per-gold ceiling; the real selector is fit on n=73 cues, leave-one-cue-out → overfitting risk (same small-data trap as fine-tuning). It'll land between ~22% (naive) and 53% (ceiling); where it lands is the open question. Augment + regularize before trusting it.
